Why we watch volcanoes breathe

Every volcano leaks gas, even while it sleeps. Reading that breath — continuously, is how NOVAC turns an invisible signal into an early warning.

Volcanic activity and its warning signals

Earth breaths out and evolves through volcanoes. Gas emissions are "telegrams from Earth's interior" whose signal can be hard to interpret, but give us clues about what happen inside, and what could happen at the surface.

Volcanoes for good and bad

Nearly a tenth of global population lives under the threat of volcanic eruptions. Humans have learned that while volcanoes may be dangerous, they also give us fertile lands, mineral and energy resources, and beautiful landscapes. The key is learning to live with volcanoes.

Gases are key to understand volcanoes

Deep in the magma there are volatiles that may be released before magma reaches the surface. Then a plume of gases such as water, carbon dioxide or sulfur dioxide is formed and travels long distances from the crater. The amount and relative composition of these gases tell us a whole lot about the volcano moves. This is golden information for reducing the risk and impact of volcanic activity.

Measuring, without risk

Capturing the signals of gas emission is super important, but it is too dangerous and expensive to measure at the crater. NOVAC was established to help volcanologists getting the signal at minimal risk, by measuring remotely, interpreting the absorption of light by volcanic gas molecules in the atmosphere.

Our shared goal

Through global collaboration, we aim at advancing safe and accurate measurement of volcanic emissions, to better understand volcanoes and reduce their risk.

That's the whole point of NOVAC

One global network, watching volcanoes' breath — so the next warning sign is never missed.
